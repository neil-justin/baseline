# PH-anchored competency framework layering

The thesis panel challenged the credibility of the platform's per-role skill lists, which were hand-curated with US O*NET / EU ESCO as informal references. We decided the Competency Framework is a six-layer, PH-anchored model: PSOC (PSA) as the occupational anchor for every catalog role; the Philippine Skills Framework (PSF, DICT/TESDA) as the primary role-level skills source for covered sectors; TESDA office qualifications plus O*NET/ESCO as *explicitly declared* gap-fillers where no PSF sector document exists (notably accounting/finance and general office administration); PSF Enabling Skills + NACE (international reference, mirroring the PSF's own contextualization methodology) for cross-cutting competencies; CHED CMOs as the academic anchor; and the DOLE-BLE National Jobs and Labor Market Forecast as demand-side justification for which roles enter the catalog.

## Considered Options

- **O*NET-SOC as primary framework** (the prior implicit approach) — rejected: O*NET is the US Department of Labor's taxonomy; no PH-thesis precedent for citing it as primary was found; the panel's credibility concern targets exactly this.
- **Single PH framework for everything** — rejected: no PH artifact covers both role-level skills and cross-cutting competencies across all in-scope sectors; PSF comes closest but has no accounting/finance or general administration coverage.

## Consequences

- Every skill in the career catalog must carry its source layer; uncovered roles must declare their gap-filler openly rather than silently mixing sources.
- Catalog role selection is justified demand-side (DOLE-BLE forecast) and classified per PSOC, not per O*NET codes.
- Research backing with primary-source URLs: `.scratch/career-readiness-platform/research/ph-competency-frameworks.md` (includes a verify-before-final-citation list).
